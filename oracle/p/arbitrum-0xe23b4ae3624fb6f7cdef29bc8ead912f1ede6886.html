Why this score

This is the protocol that lost ~$50M on 2024-10-16 to compromised multisig-signer devices, a failure mode a timelock does not defend against.

pool-admin Safe bypasses the 72h timelock

Pushed on-chain 2026-09-20 (Arbitrum Sepolia block 310697409; previously 55 / 100 / 55 with composite 69): PoolAddressesProvider.owner() is a real 72-hour TimelockController (getMinDelay 259,200s), but getPoolAdmin() is a different address, a 4-of-11 Safe, and that Safe alone gates the LendingPoolConfigurator’s onlyPoolAdmin functions: updateAToken and the two debt-token upgrades (which upgrade the token proxies), freezeReserve, setReserveFactor, caps, the interest-rate strategy and batchInitReserve. A 2026-09-20 eth_call simulation, not re-run by the scorer, passed from that Safe and reverted CALLER_NOT_POOL_ADMIN from the timelock. The Safe also holds PROPOSER and CANCELLER on the timelock and 6 of its 11 owners hold EXECUTOR, so the delay still covers the provider-level owner functions but not the token-upgrade path. It is therefore scored as a Safe with no timelock: admin key 65 (threshold of 3 or more), multisig 95 (4 × 15 + 7 × 5, capped at 100), timelock 0, composite 55; an unread pool admin or provider owner degrades to 20 / 0 / 0. getEmergencyAdmin() is a separate 1-of-5 Safe whose five owners are all owners of the 4-of-11; it can only pause, so it is disclosed and not scored. Cross-exposure stays 100 on a dated check of the pool-admin and emergency-admin Safes against the 70 root-signer groups tracked across 7 ecosystems, which found no overlap (found none, not proof that none exists). The 2024-10-16 exploit (corroborated by DefiLlama’s hacks dataset and Radiant’s own remediation docs) compromised the SIGNERS themselves via blind-signing, not the timelock mechanism, so the delay would not have stopped it. This project scores the current authority mechanism, not historical incidents against it, but discloses the context since it bears directly on how much trust to place in the admin-key dimension here.
