Why this score

Separately, the 5-of-9 guardian Safe that can cancel payloads has the same 9 signers as the guardians on Ethereum L1, Arbitrum, Base and Plasma, so one committee holds that seat on at least five chains.

PROTOCOL_GUARDIAN Safe holds POOL_ADMIN, cross-exposure: 80

Level_1 has an 86,400s delay and all payloads created so far were executed at least that long after queueing, while Level_2 reads the zero address with a 0s delay here (Ethereum L1 has a Level_2 executor with a 7-day delay). The DAO path covers the Monad-side Executor and PayloadsController, whose message originator is Ethereum’s Aave Governance V3 core (chain id 1); that Ethereum root itself was not re-verified here. Pushed on-chain 2026-09-20 (Monad testnet block 64022942; previously 65 / 100 / 50 with composite 71): the ACLManager, read live through getACLManager(), reports isPoolAdmin and isEmergencyAdmin both true for the PROTOCOL_GUARDIAN Safe, a 4-of-7 (four bare-EOA owners and three nested 1-of-3 Safes) whose 7 owners are identical to Ethereum L1’s PROTOCOL_GUARDIAN and to the emergency-admin Safes on Arbitrum, Base and Plasma. isPoolAdmin lets it upgrade every reserve’s aToken and variableDebtToken implementation at once (state-override simulation, 2026-09-19), so the 1-day Executor delay does not bind it. The Executor holds DEFAULT_ADMIN and can revoke the seat, and the Safe cannot grant roles; both are disclosed and not score inputs. The scorer therefore applies the Safe-with-no-timelock rule: admin key 65 (threshold of 3 or more, never raised above the DAO-path value), multisig 75 (4 × 15 + 3 × 5), timelock 0, composite 49. If the Safe is confirmed not to hold isPoolAdmin it falls back to the DAO-path scoring (65 / 100 / 50), and if the seat cannot be read it degrades to 20 / 20 / 0. Cross-exposure is 80 while the 9-signer guardian committee matches.
