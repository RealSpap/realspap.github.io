Lido on BNB Chain: who can change what verifies a message, and how fast?

Lido's governance on Ethereum sends messages to its executor on BNB Chain through bridge adapters: the controller allows four today (LayerZero, Hyperlane, CCIP and Wormhole), and a proposal in the forum thread would remove Wormhole and leave three. This page reads, at fixed blocks, what verifies a message on the LayerZero, Hyperlane and CCIP routes today, who can change it, and with what delay. It asks whether this is the intended setting; it does not say it is wrong. Every row can be replayed with a public RPC and no key.

Method

  • Nothing is sent. Every value is an eth_call (cast call), and every simulation is an eth_call with a --from address: nothing is signed or sent, no key is used.
  • Every value on the BNB Chain side was read on two independent public RPCs (bsc-dataseed1.bnbchain.org and bsc-rpc.publicnode.com) at the same block: 84 facts, 84 agree (78 identical, 6 refused on both sides with a different error text), none unread. The block is fixed again before each fact, because public BNB nodes keep about 128 blocks of state.
  • Each simulation is paired with a witness: the same call from the burn address 0x...dEaD, which must revert. A call that works from the holder and reverts from the witness shows that the holder is the one the contract lets act.
  • A revert from a function that does not exist is read as: the function is not there. The code of the three adapters was also searched for the selectors of owner, setDelegate, setConfig and transferOwnership: none is present.
  • "None found" means no delay was found in the contracts read. It does not mean no delay exists elsewhere.

Blocks read

  • BNB Chain: block 127,626,770 to 127,626,952, 14 October 2026, 16:29 to 16:30 UTC
  • BNB Chain, second read (13 facts: every witness call and the two Hyperlane multisig modules, 2 sources, all agreed): block 127,631,186 to 127,631,211, 14 October 2026, 17:02 UTC UTC
  • Ethereum (sender side, one source: a replay script on ethereum-rpc.publicnode.com): block 26,191,915, 14 October 2026, about 16:23 UTC

Replay it

Each call sends nothing and needs no key. The commands read the latest block, not the blocks above, so a value can differ if something changed since.

What I did not read

  • I did not read how Chainlink's network (the DON and the risk-management network) behind CCIP is composed. So I did not compare the four LayerZero verifiers with the operators of that network, which is the question asked in the forum thread.
  • The signers of the OneSig, the Safe and the bypass role are addresses. Who they are was not read and is not claimed.
  • I did not read how each verifier is administered inside (who can change its signers), nor who is behind the account that owns the Hyperlane pause switch.
  • The names of the Hyperlane validators come from the Hyperlane registry as read on 12 October and were not reloaded; the names of the LayerZero verifiers come from the LayerZero metadata API.
  • I did not read whether the two older CCIP off-ramps (versions 1.2.0 and 1.5.0) still deliver messages. I read their registration in the router, and nothing else about them.
  • No event log was read: this is state at one moment, not the history of changes.
  • The Ethereum side (the sender) was read by a single source, a replay script, not by two as on BNB Chain.
  • I did not read when the vote on the proposal is scheduled; the forum announces a later on-chain vote, without a date.
